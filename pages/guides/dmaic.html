DMAIC: five steps from problem to proof.

DMAIC is the backbone of a Lean Six Sigma project. Walk through the five phases below. One example runs through all of them: invoices that get paid late.

Walk through the phases.

Pick a phase, or use the left and right arrow keys once a tab has focus.

1. Define

Goal. Agree what problem you are solving, for whom, and what success looks like.

Key tools
Project charter, SIPOC, voice-of-the-customer notes
Typical deliverable
A one-page project charter with a problem statement, scope, goal and team.
Late invoices example
Customers wait too long for invoices to be paid. Goal: cut late invoices from 22% to under 10% in six months.
Question to ask
Who is affected, and how will we know it is better?

2. Measure

Goal. Describe the current process with data, so everyone starts from facts, not feelings.

Key tools
Process map, data collection plan, baseline measures, measurement checks
Typical deliverable
A baseline: how the process performs today, and confidence that the data can be trusted.
Late invoices example
Count invoices sent and paid over the last 12 weeks. Result: 22% paid late, on average 9 days after the due date.
Question to ask
How do we know the numbers are right?

3. Analyze

Goal. Find the real causes of the problem and test them against the data.

Key tools
Cause-and-effect diagram, 5 Whys, Pareto chart, simple statistical tests
Typical deliverable
A short list of verified root causes, ranked by impact.
Late invoices example
A Pareto chart shows that two causes explain most late payments: wrong purchase-order numbers and invoices sent to the wrong contact.
Question to ask
Is this a cause we can prove, or just an opinion?

4. Improve

Goal. Try solutions aimed at the verified causes, and check that they work before rolling out.

Key tools
Brainstorming, solution selection matrix, pilot, error-proofing
Typical deliverable
A tested solution with pilot results and a rollout plan.
Late invoices example
A pilot adds a required purchase-order field and a check of the contact before sending. Late invoices in the pilot team drop to 8%.
Question to ask
What is the smallest test that would show this works?

5. Control

Goal. Make the gain last: standard work, simple monitoring and a clear owner.

Key tools
Control chart, standard operating procedure, response plan, handover
Typical deliverable
A control plan and an owner who keeps watching the measure after the project closes.
Late invoices example
A weekly chart of late invoices goes on the team board. If it passes 10%, the owner follows the response plan.
Question to ask
Who will notice if it starts slipping, and what will they do?

The seven steps.

  1. 1. Problem statement

    Say what is wrong, where, how big and since when, with a number. No cause and no solution in it.

    Weak: “Our invoicing is slow.” Better: “In the last three months, 22% of invoices were sent more than five days after delivery.”

  2. 2. Scope

    Name where the project starts and ends in the process, and list what is out. A tight scope finishes; a loose one drifts.

    Example: in scope, from delivery confirmation to invoice sent; out of scope, payment collection.

  3. 3. Goal

    One measurable target with a date. Use the same measure as the problem statement.

    Example: reduce late invoices from 22% to under 5% by the end of the sixth month.

  4. 4. Metric

    Define exactly how you count: the unit, the formula, the data source and who measures. Agree on this before you start measuring.

    Example: late invoice = sent more than five working days after delivery confirmation, counted weekly from the billing system.

  5. 5. Team and roles

    Name the sponsor, the project leader and two to five members who know the process. Say how much time each gives.

  6. 6. Timeline

    Give a date for each DMAIC phase: Define, Measure, Analyse, Improve, Control. Leave room for the control phase; it is the one that gets squeezed.

  7. 7. Risks and assumptions

    List what could stop the project (people on leave, data you cannot get, a system change) and what you are assuming. Add who handles each.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Hiding a solution inside the problem statement (“we need a new system”).

  • A goal with no number or no date.

  • A scope that covers the whole company.

A worked example.

Sample only: a made-up company and made-up numbers.

Project
Faster invoicing at Northfield Supplies (sample)
Problem statement
In the last three months, 22% of invoices were sent more than five working days after delivery confirmation, which delays payment.
Scope
In: delivery confirmation to invoice sent, all standard orders. Out: payment collection, special contracts.
Goal
Reduce late invoices from 22% to under 5% within six months.
Metric
Share of invoices sent more than five working days after delivery confirmation, counted weekly from the billing system.
Team
Sponsor: finance manager. Leader: billing team lead. Members: two billing clerks, one warehouse coordinator, one analyst (about four hours a week each).
Timeline
Define: weeks 1 to 2. Measure: weeks 3 to 6. Analyse: weeks 7 to 10. Improve: weeks 11 to 18. Control: weeks 19 to 26.
Risks and assumptions
Quarter-end peak may hide the improvement (compare like with like). Billing data needs a weekly export from IT. Assumes the warehouse confirms deliveries the same day.
